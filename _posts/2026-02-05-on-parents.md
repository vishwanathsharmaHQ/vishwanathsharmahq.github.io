---
title: on parents
lede: The people I have known the longest, and understood the least.
date: 2026-02-05
---

My parents were people before they were parents. I have only recently begun to hold this thought without flinching. It is a strange act of imagination to picture your father at twenty-three, before he had you, before he had answers, before he had become the person you know. But the exercise is worth doing. It softens something.

What I've learned from watching my parents age is that most of the certainties I inherited from them were, on inspection, just their best guesses. They didn't know either. They were making it up as they went, exactly as I am now. This is not a criticism. It's a relief. I have permission to also not know.

The conversations I have with my parents now are shorter and more careful than the ones I had at twenty. I've stopped trying to change them. They've stopped, mostly, trying to change me. What's left is affection, and news, and the shared task of watching each other get older. It's not less than we used to have. It's just quieter.

I do not know how long I have with them. Nobody does. What I have decided is that I will not have the conversations I want to have with them "later." Later is a lie. I have started calling more often, staying on the phone longer, asking questions I would have thought were too intimate a decade ago. Most of the answers surprise me. All of them are worth having.

If you have parents, and you can, call them today. This is not a homily. This is a practical instruction. The version of you in ten years will be glad you did. The versions of them in ten years may not be around to be called.
