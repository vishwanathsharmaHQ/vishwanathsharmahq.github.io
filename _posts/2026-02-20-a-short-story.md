---
title: a short story
lede: Fiction, mostly. Names changed. The train was real.
date: 2026-02-20
---

There was a woman on the last train who fell asleep against my shoulder. I did not know her. She woke at the second-to-last station, apologized without opening her eyes fully, and got off. I watched her walk the platform until the doors closed. For a long time afterwards I thought about her, not romantically but with a kind of quiet gratitude, the way you think about a stranger who reminded you that most people are, on average, gentler than you had feared.

The train pulled away and I noticed she'd left a book on the seat. Paperback, dog-eared, the title in a language I couldn't read. I picked it up. Inside the cover, in careful pencil, someone had written a single line: "for the year I forgot to hope." I sat with it in my lap for the rest of the ride, feeling that I had been handed something without meaning to be.

I kept the book on a shelf for years. I never learned what it was. I never tried to find her; the fantasy of trying to find her was more useful to me than any real search would have been. Sometimes the most valuable gift a stranger can give you is the reminder that other people carry sentences you'll never read.

I gave the book away, eventually. I put it on a bench in a park with a note on top: "for whoever needs the year they forgot to hope." I don't know who took it. I know it was gone by the next morning, and I know I have thought about it many times since. That book is somewhere in the world, doing its work.

None of this is a story in the classical sense. Nothing much happens. A woman falls asleep, wakes, leaves. A book changes hands twice. Somewhere in there I decided to be nicer to strangers, which is the only kind of moral I trust: the kind that doesn't announce itself, the kind that only shows up in the way you behave a week later.
