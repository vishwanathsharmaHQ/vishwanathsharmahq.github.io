---
title: on change
lede: How I've stopped fighting my own reinventions.
date: 2026-03-05
---

I used to think I was a fixed person with fixed opinions and that any evidence to the contrary was a failure of will. This turned out to be wrong. I have been three or four people already in this life, and I suspect I will be a couple more before it's over.

The person I was at twenty-two would find the person I am now boring. The person I was at thirty would find the person I am now impossibly earnest. The person I am now finds both of them a bit exhausting. All three of us are correct, from where we're standing.

The mistake was thinking that identity was a monument. It isn't. It's a garden. Some of the plants will die. Some of them will surprise you. Your job is not to keep it all alive at once but to notice what's still growing and give it the water.

Changing your mind in public is embarrassing and worth it. Every time I've walked something back — publicly, without excuses — I've felt lighter, and the people I care about have moved closer, not further. The people who move away were never on my side; they were on the side of the old opinion, and they were never going to travel with me.

The internet punishes changing your mind, or at least pretends to. The old post surfaces. Someone quotes you back to yourself. In practice, what I've found is that if you own the change plainly — "I thought this, I don't anymore, here's why" — most people respect it. The ones who don't were looking for a reason to be annoyed with you anyway.

I am trying to be the kind of person my future selves will still recognize. That's the only continuity I owe them. Not the same opinions, not the same tastes, not the same haircut. Just a thread of care about being honest and paying attention. If that thread holds, the rest can change as often as it likes.
