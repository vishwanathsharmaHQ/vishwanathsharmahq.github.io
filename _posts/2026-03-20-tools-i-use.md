title: tools i use
lede: The short list of things that pay their rent.
date: 2026-03-20
---

I've tried a lot of tools. Most of them get uninstalled inside a month. The ones that stay have earned it. Here is the current list, with the caveat that any list like this is out of date the moment it's published.

A plain text editor. I've cycled through many. What matters is that it opens fast, doesn't nag me about updates, and gets out of my way. Any editor that requires a tutorial is one I don't want. My relationship with an editor should be like my relationship with a pencil: unremarkable, dependable, forgettable.

A single notebook. Paper, unlined, small enough to fit in a jacket pocket. I don't take extensive notes; I take one note per thing worth remembering. If a day produces no notes, the day probably didn't have much I wanted to remember. If a day produces ten, I'll be busy the next morning.

A terminal I've customized as little as possible. Every hour spent tweaking a prompt is an hour not spent doing the work the terminal exists for. Fewer plugins, fewer aliases, fewer surprises. When I sit down at someone else's machine I don't want to feel disabled.

A camera on my phone that I've stopped fighting. It takes worse pictures than a real camera. That's fine. It takes them at the exact moment I would have missed with a real camera. The best camera is the one you'll actually raise to your face.

A calendar with everything on it, including the things I don't want to do. If it's not on the calendar it won't happen; if it is, it usually will. I've stopped believing in the mental to-do list. The list evaporates by lunch. The calendar keeps its word.

A reading app that syncs across devices, so I can pick up where I left off. The specific app matters less than the syncing. The friction of losing your place is what makes people stop reading. Remove the friction.

Everything else is negotiable. The list gets shorter every year, and I am not sure whether that's a sign I'm figuring out what I need or a sign I'm becoming easier to please. Either way I don't miss the abandoned tools. If I did, I'd have gone back for them.
