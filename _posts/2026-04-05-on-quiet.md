---
title: on quiet
lede: An underrated skill, and a scarce resource.
date: 2026-04-05
---

The hardest thing to do in modern life is nothing. Sit in a chair without a phone or a book or a podcast, and see how long you last. Ninety seconds is a real achievement. Five minutes is a discipline. Twenty minutes is a practice. Nobody teaches this. Everybody could use it.

The mind, given nothing to consume, will begin to produce. It's a strange machine that way. The best ideas I've ever had came in the shower or on a walk or in the twenty minutes after I woke up but before I moved. What all of these have in common is that I was doing nothing, and no one had my attention.

Quiet is not the absence of sound. It's the absence of intrusion. You can have quiet in a busy café if no one is trying to get you to react. You can have loudness in a silent room if your phone is on the table, threatening to buzz. Choose your quiet by input, not by decibel.

The pandemic taught me something about quiet that I keep forgetting. For months I had a great deal of it, and I did not always use it well. I filled it with noise of a different kind — news, feeds, calls, the anxious murmur of trying to keep up with something moving too fast. The quiet was there. I didn't inhabit it. This has become a warning I give myself: quiet only works if you say yes to it.

Now, I try to hold one hour a day where I'm not consuming anything. No text, no audio, no video, no book. Just me and whatever the mind serves up. Some days it serves up nothing and I sit there like an idiot. Some days it serves up an idea I've been chasing for weeks. The success rate doesn't matter. The habit matters. Quiet is a room you have to visit before it will let you in.
