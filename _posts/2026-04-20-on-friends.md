---
title: on friends
lede: The people who make the rest of it worth it.
date: 2026-04-20
---

The friends I keep are the ones who ask a second question. Anyone can ask the first one — "how have you been?" — and most people do. The second question is rarer. It's the one that says, "I heard you, and I want to hear more." A friendship, over time, is made of second questions.

I've made most of my closest friends in the middle of something else. On projects, on trips, in classes. Very few came from trying to make friends. This has practical implications: if you want more friends, go do more things. Don't go to networking events. Go build something with people and let the friendship be a byproduct.

Time zones and distance are the great enemies of adult friendship. There is no substitute for being in the same room. Video calls are better than nothing, but they are not the same as sitting on a couch with a friend and letting the silences be silences. The best friendships survive distance because both people have committed to the theater of returning. You will see each other again, and you know it.

I've lost friends and it hasn't always been anyone's fault. Sometimes we grow at different rates in different directions. Sometimes the friendship was tied to a life that ended. The best you can do is be grateful for the years and stop demanding permanence from something that was, by its nature, seasonal.

The old idea, that you have a fixed capacity for close friends, has felt true in my life. I think it's around six. That's not many. It means you have to choose. It means you can't be casually available to everyone. It means friendship, at some point, becomes a resource question. Give the resource to the people who ask the second question.

I try to send more voice notes than I used to. They are a small gift. They cost you three minutes and they give someone a friend in their pocket for the rest of the day. The medium matters more than we let on. A text is a signal; a voice is a person.
