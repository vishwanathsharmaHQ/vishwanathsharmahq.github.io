---
title: mornings
lede: In defense of being awake before the world is.
date: 2026-05-08
---

The best hour of the day is the one before anyone else needs anything from me. There is a particular quiet at six in the morning that I have never been able to find at any other hour. It isn't the absence of sound — the city is already moving, birds are already at it — it's the absence of demand. Nothing is asking to be answered. The inbox has not yet caught up with the fact that I am awake.

I make coffee. I sit near a window if there is one. I read a page from whatever is nearest, not to accomplish anything but to warm the language up. If I write in the morning, the sentences come out cleaner than they will at any other point in the day. I don't know why. Maybe the brain hasn't had time to insist on being clever yet.

I've tried to be a morning person on purpose and it never works. I've tried to be a night person on purpose and it works for exactly one week before I get sick. What I've settled into is: sleep when I'm tired, wake when I'm done, and try not to schedule anything demanding before I've been vertical for an hour. Sometimes this means I write at six. Sometimes it means I write at ten. The category of "productive time" has more to do with alertness than with the clock.

The single greatest change to my mornings has been to keep the phone in another room until I've had coffee. This sounds obvious and small. It is neither. Reading a screen before your eyes have adjusted to being open is a way of losing the day before it starts. The screen sets a jangling pace and you spend the next twelve hours trying to catch up to it.

I don't recommend routines to other people. Routines are personal, and someone else's is usually irrelevant to your life. But I do recommend paying attention to which hour of your day is the hour when the sentences come. Protect that hour. It's the whole point.
