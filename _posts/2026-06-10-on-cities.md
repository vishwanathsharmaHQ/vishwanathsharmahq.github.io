---
title: on cities
lede: A love letter, with reservations.
date: 2026-06-10
---

A city is a large group of strangers who have agreed, mostly, not to kill each other. When you say it that way it sounds thin. It isn't. The agreement holds most days. It's a small miracle, renewed at every crosswalk and every subway car, and the fact that it holds at all is one of the underrated achievements of our species.

What I love about a city is the compression of choice. In a small town you might know everyone, but you know them at one pace. In a city, you can walk three blocks and change your mind about what kind of evening you want to have. That freedom is expensive and worth it.

What I dislike about a city is the way it can make you performative. You start dressing for strangers and forgetting your friends. You start optimizing for how you look in transit. If you're not careful, you become a character in the background of someone else's story, and you forget you have a story of your own.

The good cities are the ones with sidewalks wide enough to walk two abreast without apologizing. The great cities have benches you can sit on without buying anything. The best cities have both, plus a bookstore that stays open past ten.

I have lived in three cities long enough to call each of them home. None of them feel like home when I return. That's not a complaint. Home is a specific window of time you happened to spend somewhere, and cities are ruthless about renovating their windows.

I think a lot about the small choices cities make about their surfaces. The color of the paving stones. The width of the tree pits. Whether the streetlights lean toward the sidewalk or the road. These are the details that decide whether a city welcomes you or tolerates you. Most cities tolerate. The rare few welcome. You know within a day which kind you're in.
