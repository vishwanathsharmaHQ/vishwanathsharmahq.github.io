A slow-moving log of what's on the desk. I'll add short reactions here as I finish things, and longer pieces will graduate into their own books on the shelf. I read too many things at once and finish about half of them. The other half sit in a stack, patient, waiting for me to be ready.

Currently: an old collection of Berger's essays, a slim book on Japanese aesthetics that I keep starting over, and a stack of engineering papers that I'm reading two paragraphs at a time before bed. Berger is the one I keep coming back to. He writes about paintings the way I want to write about paragraphs — as if each one is a small country worth spending an afternoon in.

Recently finished: a memoir about learning to farm in the north of England. The prose was steady and unshowy, the kind that only looks easy. There's a chapter about a stone wall that I've read three times and I still don't know why it moved me. Good writing does that. It refuses to explain itself and you agree to stop asking.

Ongoing project: I'm trying to read one poem a day, out loud, before I open my laptop. It's changing something about how I hear sentences. Poetry teaches prose to breathe. Every writer I love has some quiet debt to a poet they can't quite name.

What I'm avoiding: anything called "the definitive guide to." The word "definitive" is a tell. Anyone who has read enough on a subject knows there is no definitive anything. There are angles. There are seasons. There are the books that were right for you five years ago and would be wrong for you now.
