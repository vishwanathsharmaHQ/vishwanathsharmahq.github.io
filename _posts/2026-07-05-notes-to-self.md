title: notes to self
lede: Small things worth remembering.
date: 2026-07-05
---

A running list of things I want to be able to find again. Some are quotes, some are half-thoughts, some are questions I don't yet have the vocabulary to ask properly. I keep them here because a phone notes app is a graveyard and a paper notebook is a hostage situation. At least here, if the note is any good, it might do work in someone else's head.

Note: the interesting people I know are almost never the ones who are trying to be interesting. They are the ones who are trying to be honest. There's a kind of quiet attention in them that pulls stories out of a room. If you want to be that person, start by paying attention to the person in front of you as if you'll be quizzed on them later.

Note: most of my anxieties are about things I don't want to do but haven't yet admitted I don't want to do. The moment I say the words out loud, the anxiety collapses into a decision, and decisions are cheap. Anxiety is the tax you pay on undecided decisions.

Note: the shortest path from here to a better life is almost always: sleep more, drink water, walk further, call someone I love. I keep looking for a fifth answer. There isn't one.

Note: I have never regretted being kind, but I have often regretted being clever. Kindness ages well. Cleverness sours by morning.

Note: the best conversations happen when neither person is performing. Performance is exhausting to watch and even more exhausting to do. If you catch yourself performing, stop. Sit quietly for a minute. The room will forgive you.

Note: I want to write more like I talk to my closest friends: direct, unhurried, unafraid of being caught not knowing. Every draft where I sound like a magazine is a draft I need to redo.

Note: whenever I say "I don't have time," I really mean "I have not made time." The distinction is small and enormous. The first is a fact about the world; the second is a decision about my priorities. Only one of them is something I can change.

Note: if I could give my younger self one piece of advice, it would be to start writing sooner. Not because I have anything worth saying that I didn't have then — I had less, in fact — but because writing is a muscle, and I could have had ten more years of practice by now.
